# Lab 08 — Plans to outline during a data breach

A data breach occurs when unauthorised parties get access to private, sensitive or
otherwise protected information — Social Security numbers, bank details, medical
records, corporate secrets, intellectual property and financial data.

A data breach response plan (also called a data breach response guide) is a
framework that sets out the duties of everyone involved in handling a breach and
the actions they take if one happens.

## Assessment and planning

Establish a list of important stakeholders — IT, legal, communications, human
resources and executive leadership. Identify potential weaknesses in the company's
data systems and rank them by likelihood and potential impact. Set specific
objectives for the response strategy, such as preserving client information,
avoiding reputational harm, and adhering to applicable laws.

## Development of the plan

Assign specialised responsibilities to groups or individuals: technical research,
communicating with affected parties, legal compliance, and public relations.
Establish procedures for sharing information internally and externally during and
after a breach, including who should be informed and how.

Provide thorough, step-by-step guidelines for handling different kinds of data
breaches — how to contain the breach, determine the damage, and get systems back
up and running normally. Include any applicable legal requirements for reporting
data breaches in the relevant jurisdictions.

## Tests and training

Test the plan regularly by simulating breach scenarios, so team members can
rehearse their duties and gaps can be found. Train employees on their duties and
responsibilities if a breach is discovered — including how to spot common security
risks and what to do when one is found.

## Implementation

As soon as a breach happens, launch the response plan under the established
protocols. Act fast to stop the intrusion and prevent further unauthorised access.
Investigate thoroughly to find the root cause and extent: which data was
compromised and how.

Communicate with affected parties — consumers, staff, regulators and law
enforcement — as required by law and company policy. Reduce the impact by offering
affected parties credit monitoring or by putting extra security measures in place
to prevent a repeat. Conduct a post-incident review once the breach is fixed to
capture lessons learned and adjust the plan.

## Continuous improvement

Review and update the plan regularly so it stays effective as technology and
security risks change. Use lessons from previous incidents to strengthen the plan
and the company's overall security posture.

## In summary

Data breaches are now a matter of when, not if. Preparedness and resilience are
what limit the damage: a plan is only the first step — ongoing testing,
instruction and development are what make it work when a real breach hits.
